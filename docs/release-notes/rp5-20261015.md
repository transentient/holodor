## Device

Retroid Pocket 5 (Snapdragon 865), both the original and Visionox screen revisions. You can run Holodor straight off a microSD card alongside Android, or install it to internal storage for faster load times.

This release rebuilds the audio stack, fixes USB-C docks, and cuts battery drain during sleep.

## What works

- Steam Big Picture, built-in controls, rumble, Wi-Fi, and sleep/wake.
- Sleep power management: the audio codec, gamepad microcontroller, Hall-effect stick sensors, RGB LED controllers, and 5V fan boost converter all power down during sleep and come right back on wake.
- Built-in speakers and the 3.5mm headphone jack (with automatic plug detection). The volume slider scales evenly: 50% is a comfortable everyday level (and the default on first boot), while 100% drives the amplifiers at full output.
- The display runs at 60 Hz by default to save battery and switches to 120 Hz whenever a game's frame limit calls for it (use the per-game "120 Hz Screen" toggle in Pocknix Control).
- Games stay pinned to the fast CPU cores while background work like shader compilation runs on the smaller cores.
- Desktop Mode includes an on-screen keyboard on the taskbar.
- USB-C docks: 1080p 60 Hz video and audio over HDMI or DisplayPort. Audio switches automatically when you plug in or unplug the dock, without cutting off the first sound. Booting with a dock already plugged in works too.
- Sleep on a dock: the device sleeps and wakes on the dock with the picture, charging and the dock's ethernet coming back.
- Docking and undocking with ethernet does not screw up networking.
- Unity games that use H.264, VP9, or Theora for intro videos play them out of the box (Hollow Knight still skips its intro video, which uses a different format).

## Known issues

- Heavy 3D games run at 10 to 30 fps and the device gets warm. Check the [tested games list](../games-rp5.md) for frame rates and settings that help.
- DirectX 12 games do not run on the Snapdragon 865 GPU.
- Native Linux x86 games do not run; Steam runs everything through Proton on ARM.
- Holodor uses Valve's ARM64 Steam client, which Valve builds for its own hardware and does not officially support on third-party devices. Bugs inside the Steam client itself are outside our control.
- Steam needs free disk space while unpacking downloads. Queuing several large installs at once on a nearly full drive can stall the system until you reboot.
- Some games sit on a blank or loading screen for a couple of minutes the very first time you launch them (Dark Souls Remastered takes about two minutes on a white screen before the title appears).
- At 100% volume the speakers hit the hardware amplifier limit, so loud games can clip. Android gets about 5 dB more volume out of the speakers using Qualcomm's proprietary speaker-protection DSP, which Holodor does not use yet.
- Some HDMI dongles occasionally fail to send their display info when plugged in, leaving the external screen at 640x480 with no audio. Unplugging and replugging the dock clears it up.
- Dock ethernet occasionally wakes from sleep at USB 2.0 speed (around 300 Mbit/s instead of gigabit). Unplug and replug the dock if you need full gigabit speed back.
